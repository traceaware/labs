EU product passport readiness

AWARE™ already holds the supply chain data a textile product passport will need. The EU published the passport format, registry and standards in 2026, and we are building them into AWARE™.

The data

  • Material composition

    Fibre composition with percentages, built up from every material in the product's supply chain.

    In place

  • Recycled and sustainable content

    Each sustainable material claim is backed by a source declaration and proof provided by the supplier.

    In place

  • Origin of each production step

    Company, facility and country for every step, from raw material to finished product.

    In place

  • Chain of custody

    Every transfer and transformation is recorded, with quantities in and out on a blockchain record.

    In place

  • Certificates and test reports

    Facility certificates (such as GOTS, GRS, BSCI) and shipment documents (transaction certificates, lab reports), each linked to the file.

    In place

  • Care instructions

    Care information set by the brand and shown on each passport.

    In place

  • Carbon footprint

    Emissions in kg CO2e for each production step. The method will follow the textile rules when they are published.

    In place

  • End-of-life information

    Planned once the textile rules define the information required.

    Planned

  • Substances of concern

    Not collected yet. Planned once the textile rules set the list of substances.

    Planned

The format

How a passport is identified, structured and registered. These rules are new, and this is where our work is focused.

  • Unique product identifier

    Every passport has its own web address today. It will move to the EU format, built on the product's GTIN.

    In progress

  • Passport level

    AWARE™ passports cover each production batch and the transactions between suppliers. Each passport will state its level: model, batch or item.

    In progress

  • GTIN

    Brands already hold GTINs for their products. We are adding a field to store them.

    Planned

  • QR code

    Every passport has a QR code. Support for the GS1 Digital Link format will be added.

    In place

  • Passport in the EU data model

    Passports open as web pages today. A JSON version in the EU data model, with passport ID and status, is next.

    Planned

  • Manufacturer details and identifier

    Full company details are held. GLN and EORI numbers will be added on request.

    In place

  • Every company in the supply chain

    Every supplier has a company profile with its registration details. GLN and EORI numbers will be added on request.

    In place

  • Facility identifiers

    Address, registration number and certificates are held for each facility. GLN and Open Supply Hub IDs will be added on request.

    In place

  • Importer and EORI number

    Not collected yet. Planned alongside the operator identifiers.

    Planned

  • Customs commodity code

    Not collected yet. Planned for each product.

    Planned

  • Compliance documents

    Certificates and test reports are linked to each passport. EU declarations will be added when the textile rules define them.

    In place

  • Public and restricted views

    Each passport will show the public what the rules require and share protected data only with authorised parties.

    Planned

  • Registration with the EU registry

    AWARE™ is enrolling with the EU registry and will register passports through its API.

    In progress

  • Supply chain events

    The AWARE™ ledger records every transfer and transformation. It maps onto EPCIS events, and an export is planned.

    In progress

GS1 standards

AWARE™ already records what the GS1 standards describe: products, batches, companies, facilities and every step between them. Today though we use our own identifiers. Moving to meet the GS1 standard means collecting the numbers brands and suppliers hold from our users and formatting passport links to meet GS1 rules. The data itself stays the same. We are currently researching the best way to do this for when a client requires GS1 compliance.

When textile passports become mandatory

February 2027Battery passports become mandatory, the first product group.
Q4 2027The Commission plans to adopt the textile rules.
Q2 2029 at the earliestOur estimate. Businesses get at least 18 months after the rules are adopted.
